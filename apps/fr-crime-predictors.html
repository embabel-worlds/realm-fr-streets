What predicts crime in France?

Recorded crime for every French commune whose figure the Ministère de l'Intérieur publishes (SSMSI, 2016–2025), modelled against income, poverty, density, youth, immigrant share and population. Switch predictors on and off and watch what survives. Association across places, never a statement about people.

The model

      How it works

      1. The data

      • Recorded crime: SSMSI's communal base (Ministère de l'Intérieur, Licence Ouverte), 15 indicators, 2016–2025, read through data.gouv.fr's tabular API by the fr-streets realm. A commune's figure is published only after more than 5 facts in each of 3 consecutive years. Below that it is suppressed, and this app never shows or ranks it. Rates are per 1,000 residents (burglary: per 1,000 dwellings), counted where the offence was recorded. Cached a week.
      • Median standard of living and poverty rate: INSEE Filosofi 2023, all communes in one Melodi call. Poverty is published for about 5,200 communes. Cached a week.
      • Age and immigration: INSEE census 2023. "Immigré" means born abroad and not French at birth. France collects no ethnic statistics. Cached a week.
      • Density and names: geo.api.gouv.fr (population ÷ surface). Cached a week.
      • Your places: the FrPlace entries in your world, with per-place INSEE, crime and mayor (Répertoire national des élus) lookups.

      2. The questions

      The app calls five saved views by name through gateway.view.run. It contains no Cypher of its own.

      • FrModelRows(indicator, year, minPopulation): the model's design matrix. One row per commune in the fit, with the crime rate and all six predictors unrounded. It loads once per indicator, year and floor. The fit (ordinary least squares on standardised variables), each predictor's solo correlation (Pearson r with a 95% Fisher-z interval; "detectable" means the interval excludes 0), the outliers (the three largest positive and negative residuals) and the communes table are then all computed in this page. That is why ticking a predictor is instant.
      • FrWhatBestPredictsCrime(indicator, year, minPopulation, and the six predictor switches): the same fit computed by the engine's regress(). It is called only when you press "Check against the engine", and the page shows whether R² and every beta agree.
      • FrCrimeLeague(indicator, year, minPopulation, limit = 25): the highest published rates.
      • FrPlaceDossier(): one row per watched place.
      • FrCrimeAtMyPlaces(year, baseline = 2019): every indicator at every watched place. The app shows the selected one.

      Both model views start the same way. They read one crime "slice" (indicator, year and population floor, sent to data.gouv as its own filters) and the six whole-country covariate tables. They then group everything by INSEE code in one pass, and keep only communes where every predictor is present. That is why the commune set does not change when you switch predictors.

      The page's fit was checked against regress() for burglary 2025 above 10,000 inhabitants. Over 1,003 communes, R², all six betas, all six named outliers and all six solo correlations with their intervals were identical.

      3. When it looks wrong

      • A panel says it failed: a source or the engine did not answer, and that is not the same as "nothing found". The first load of a new indicator, year or floor can take a minute while sources refill. Press Run again.
      • "No communes": nothing passed the filters. Small floors and rare indicators (e.g. Vols avec armes) leave few published communes.
      • A low R² is the honest answer. Open data holds economic and structural variables, not the policing, reporting and opportunity factors behind much of the rest.
      • Suburbs topping burglary is expected. The rate is per dwelling, and detached-house communes near big cities are recorded as burgled more.
      • A place shows no rate: SSMSI suppressed it for that year. The app leaves it empty and never substitutes the département average.
      • An arrondissement has no mayor: the élus file lists commune mayors only.

      4. What made this possible

      • Four public sources are joined on the INSEE code inside one query (FrModelRows). There is no warehouse and no ETL job. The engine's regress() is available on demand as the reference fit, and the page refits locally because six predictors over about 1,000 communes is a millisecond calculation.
      • The crime slice is fetched with the filters the source applies itself: 1,089 of the file's 5.2 million rows for burglary 2025 above 10,000 inhabitants, in 6 pages. It is then cached for a week, as are the covariates.
